Phospholipids are often treated as just an emulsifier. They do much more: they are building blocks of every cell membrane, they help the gut digest fat, and they support the liver, the gut wall and the immune system. The response in the animal depends on species, age, diet, phospholipid source and inclusion strategy.

## What a phospholipid is

A phospholipid has an **amphipathic** structure: a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and hydrophobic (fat-loving) tails. This dual affinity is what lets it organise cell membranes and stabilise fat in the watery environment of the intestine.

## How phospholipids improve fat utilisation

<ol class="nw-steps">
<li><b>Fat droplet</b><span>A large oil or fat globule.</span></li>
<li><b>Emulsification</b><span>Smaller, stabilised droplets.</span></li>
<li><b>Lipase</b><span>More surface is exposed to the fat-digesting enzyme.</span></li>
<li><b>Micelle</b><span>Digested fat is carried in micelles.</span></li>
<li><b>Enterocyte</b><span>Intestinal cells take up the fat.</span></li>
</ol>

Phospholipids complement the animal's own bile by stabilising the boundary between fat and water. This may be most useful in broilers at an early age and high growth rate, and its relevance depends on diet factors such as fat source, saturation, free fatty acids and inclusion level.

## Ruminants: a different digestive context

In ruminants, dietary fat is extensively metabolised by rumen microbes before it is absorbed, so the mechanism changes.

<div class="nw-compare">
<div><b>Poultry</b><ol><li>Dietary fat</li><li>Emulsified in the intestine</li><li>Lipase and micelles</li><li>Taken up by intestinal cells</li></ol></div>
<div><b>Ruminants</b><ol><li>Dietary fat</li><li>Metabolised by rumen microbes; saturated or rumen-bypass fats are needed to exit the rumen</li><li>Phospholipids feed rumen microflora and supply phosphatidyl choline</li><li>Fat mobilised from the liver after calving; lipoproteins carry it to the mammary gland</li></ol></div>
</div>

Phosphatidyl choline is the key nutrient here. It helps mobilise liver fat after calving, helping to prevent ketosis and fatty liver, and it is built into the lipoproteins that improve milk and milk fat production. Milk is an emulsion, so choline is replenished through regular phospholipid supplementation.
